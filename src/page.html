Pressure Well.

Paint notes into a pattern table. It is a real synthesizer — the scope shows what it actually computes.

What this is

A pattern table: 16 beats across, 36 pitches up and down. Tap or drag to put notes in. Press Play.

Nothing here is a recording. Each note is computed from 440 × 2^((n−69)/12) — that is where the pitch comes from — and all the notes at a beat are added together, sample by sample.

The wiggly line under the grid is the actual output samples. It is worth watching because two things you can make happen are visible in it:

  • Fill a whole column and you hear a chord. The line gets busy because 12 waves are being summed — and it stays inside the box, because the mix is level-checked.
  • Switch waveform to square and high notes get a harsh buzz the triangle doesn't have. That is aliasing: a square wave needs infinitely many harmonics, and a sampler at 44100 Hz cannot make them. Turn on band-limit and the buzz goes away — the scope shows why, because the wave stops having sharp corners.

The link updates as you play, so the exact score you are hearing is shareable.
